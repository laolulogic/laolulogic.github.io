A lead fills in a form on Zillow at 8:47 pm. They're excited, they're on their phone, and they've just contacted three agents. The agent who reaches them first, with something useful to say, usually gets the conversation. Everyone else gets voicemail.

That's speed to lead. Here's how to build it into your CRM so it happens every time, not only when someone's watching.

Why the first five minutes matter

Online leads are at peak intent the moment they submit. Within an hour they've moved on to dinner, another agent or another listing. Studies of web leads have shown for years that contact rates drop sharply after the first few minutes. In my own client work, the pattern is the same: the teams that respond fastest book more appointments from the same lead spend.

The problem is rarely effort. It's that leads arrive at all hours, land in a CRM nobody is watching and wait for someone to notice them.

Step 1: automate the first touch

The first message should go out in seconds, without a human. Set your CRM to send a short, personal-sounding text and email the moment a lead is created:

Hi Sarah, it's Mike from Coastal Homes. Got your request about 14 Palm Street. Are you looking to see it this week, or still early in your search?

Keep it short, use their first name, reference what they asked about and end with an easy question. In Follow Up Boss this is an action plan triggered by the lead source; in Lofty it's a smart plan; in GoHighLevel it's a workflow.

Step 2: make calling new leads the default

  • Create a call task due in 5 minutes for the assigned agent.
  • Build a "New, not yet contacted" smart list that sits at the top of every agent's day.
  • Send an instant alert by text or app notification with the lead's name, source and property.

Step 3: route leads to someone available

Speed to lead fails when leads go to a busy or offline agent. Use round robin for general leads, source-based rules for portals and a first-to-claim option for teams that move fast. Then add a safety net: if a lead isn't contacted within your limit, it's reassigned or moved to a pond. I explain the setup in Follow Up Boss ponds and lead routing.

Step 4: cover nights and weekends

Most portal leads arrive outside office hours. Two options work well:

  • An after-hours text that sets expectations and offers a booking link.
  • An AI agent that replies, answers basic questions and books a call for the morning. See AI lead qualification.

Step 5: measure it

Track average time to first call and the percentage of leads contacted within five minutes. Review it weekly. When teams can see the number, it improves on its own.
