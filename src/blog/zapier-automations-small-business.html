You don't need fifty automations. You need the right ten or twelve. These are the Zapier workflows I build most often for small businesses, grouped by what they save.

Leads and sales

  1. Form or ad lead to CRM, tagged by source with an owner assigned.
  2. Instant lead alert to the right person by text, Slack or email.
  3. Booking to CRM: a new Calendly or Acuity booking creates or updates the contact.
  4. AI email triage: incoming emails classified and new enquiries logged as leads (see the case study).

Marketing

  1. New contacts to your email list from every form, booking and inbox.
  2. Review requests sent automatically after a job or purchase.
  3. Post-purchase follow-up for e-commerce (see Klaviyo post-purchase flows).

Operations and money

  1. Payment to invoice: a Stripe payment creates the invoice in your accounting app.
  2. New client folder created in Google Drive with templates copied in.
  3. Weekly schedule dispatch: booking links emailed to your client list every Friday (see the case study).
  4. Status sync between systems, like loan updates into your CRM (see the case study).
  5. Weekly report of leads, bookings and revenue to your inbox.

A real example

For a brand strategy consultant, six automations across eight apps connected Calendly, Google Forms, Tally and Gmail to Kit, created a Drive folder for every booking and turned Stripe payments into invoices automatically (read the case study).

Build them to last

Use filters to skip junk, "find before create" to avoid duplicates and alerts on failure. And if a zap runs thousands of times a month, check whether Make or n8n would be cheaper.
