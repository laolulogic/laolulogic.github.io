Lead routing decides who gets each lead. Ponds decide what happens when nobody works it. Together they're the difference between a team that responds in minutes and a CRM full of leads nobody owns.

Here's the blueprint I designed for a US brokerage with appointment setters and senior strategists.

Choose a routing model

ModelBest for
Round robinFair distribution across a team
By sourceSpecialists for portals, sellers or rentals
By area or zip codeAgents who own a territory
First to claimFast teams that compete for leads

Most teams combine them: source rules first, then round robin within each group.

Add a "Shark Tank" pond

If a lead isn't contacted within your limit (48 hours in the brokerage blueprint), it moves to a pond where any agent can claim it. Agents learn quickly that unworked leads don't stay theirs, and good leads never sit idle.

Route VIPs differently

High-value leads, like investors or luxury sellers, should skip the queue and go straight to your senior agents or strategists, with an instant alert.

Make routing visible with smart lists

Routing only works if agents act. Build smart lists they work left to right every morning, starting with a 5-minute speed-to-lead list. See the full list in my Follow Up Boss setup checklist.

Start the right action plan automatically

Each source should trigger its own follow-up. For a Chicago brokerage, a tag-based automation starts the Facebook leads action plan the moment a lead arrives, with run-once rules so nobody is double-enrolled (read the case study).

Review routing monthly

Check contact rates by agent and by source, and adjust. Routing isn't set-and-forget; it's how you manage the team. Read the full brokerage blueprint case study, or ask about monthly CRM management.
