Text messages get read. That's why they work for follow-up, and why getting them wrong hurts your brand fast. Here's how I set up SMS follow-up that gets replies and stays compliant.

1. Consent first

  • Add clear SMS consent wording to every form.
  • Keep a record of when and how each contact opted in.
  • Register your number for A2P 10DLC if you text US contacts (see the A2P guide).

2. Write like a person

Good follow-up texts are short, personal and end with one easy question.

Hi Maria, it's Jordan from Lakeside Realty. Saw you saved the house on Elm St. Want me to set up a quick showing this weekend?
  • Use their first name and yours.
  • Reference what they did or asked.
  • One question, no links in the first message.

3. Timing

  • Reply instantly to a fresh request; that's what they expect.
  • Otherwise text during normal hours in their time zone.
  • Space follow-ups out; no more than one unanswered text a day early on.

4. Detect replies and stop automation

Once a lead replies, automated texts should stop and a person should take over. In my mortgage broker case study, replies move the lead forward, silent leads get one follow-up after a day, and anyone who asks to stop is set to Do Not Disturb with the team notified.

5. Mix channels

Text for speed, email for detail, calls for conversations. My 12-month follow-up plan shows how the three work together.

6. Measure

Track reply rate, opt-out rate and appointments booked from texts. A rising opt-out rate means your messages are too frequent or too salesy.
