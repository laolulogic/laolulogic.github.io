The moment between "yes" and "we've started" is where clients form their first real impression, and where most businesses are slowest. A client signs, then waits for an invoice, a folder, a questionnaire and a kickoff link while someone works through a checklist. Onboarding automation fixes that.

What to automate

  1. Intake: one form captures everything you need.
  2. Payment: confirmation moves the client forward automatically.
  3. Folders and documents: a client folder with templates copied in.
  4. Welcome: email and text with next steps and a kickoff booking link.
  5. Access: portal, course or tool access granted instantly.
  6. CRM: stage updated, owner assigned, tasks created.
  7. Reminders: nudges if the client hasn't completed a step.

Example 1: a consulting agency in n8n

A consulting and talent agency spent hours on every new client. I built an n8n engine where one form submission logs the lead, branches by inquiry type, adds an AI fit score (advisory only), creates Google Drive folders and documents, schedules calendar events, sends reminder sequences and posts activity to Notion (read the case study).

Example 2: payments and access in GoHighLevel

For course creators and agencies, an order form triggers onboarding, payment confirmation sends a welcome text and paid course access is granted the moment payment clears (read the case study).

Keep the human parts human

Automate the admin, not the relationship. The kickoff call, the personal welcome video and the first check-in should still come from you; automation just makes sure they happen on time.

Where to start

Write your current onboarding as a checklist. Every step that's the same for every client is a candidate. Then choose the tool: see Zapier vs Make vs n8n, or book a call and I'll map it with you.
