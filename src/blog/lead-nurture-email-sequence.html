Most nurture emails get ignored because they read like marketing. The ones that get replies read like a person checking in. Here's how I write sequences that start conversations, and the template I adapt for clients.

The five rules

  1. Short. Under 100 words. People read on phones.
  2. Plain text. No banners or heavy design in follow-up emails.
  3. One idea per email. One tip, one question, one offer.
  4. End with an easy question they can answer in five words.
  5. Stop when they reply. Nothing kills trust like an automated email after a real conversation.

A 5-email short-term sequence

Email 1 (instant): confirm you got their request and ask one qualifying question.

Subject: Your question about [topic]
Hi Ana, thanks for reaching out. Quick question so I can point you in the right direction: is this something you're hoping to sort out this month, or later in the year?

Email 2 (day 2): a useful tip related to what they asked.

Email 3 (day 5): a short proof point, like a client result.

Email 4 (day 9): handle the most common objection.

Email 5 (day 14): the "should I close your file?" email. It often gets the most replies.

Then move to long-term nurture

Leads who don't reply aren't lost. Move them to a monthly sequence with genuinely useful content. In one GoHighLevel build, a 30-email long-term series runs for months, and an AI booking bot takes over the moment a lead engages (read the case study).

Protect your deliverability

  • Only email people who gave permission.
  • Remove bounces and unsubscribes automatically.
  • Authenticate your domain (SPF, DKIM and DMARC).
  • Ramp up slowly when emailing an old list (see database reactivation).
