If someone on your team copies leads from email notifications into the CRM, you're losing time and, sooner or later, leads. Every lead source can feed your CRM automatically. Here's how I set it up.

Option 1: native integrations

Start here. Most CRMs connect directly to Facebook Lead Ads, popular form builders and real estate portals. Native connections are usually the most reliable and need the least maintenance.

Option 2: Zapier or Make

When there's no native connection, Zapier or Make fills the gap. A typical flow:

  1. Trigger: new form submission or new Facebook lead.
  2. Validate: check the email and phone look real; filter out spam.
  3. Find or create: look up the contact first so you don't create duplicates.
  4. Tag and assign: add the source, assign an owner, start the follow-up plan.
  5. Notify: alert the right person instantly.

Not sure which tool? See Zapier vs Make vs n8n.

Option 3: webhooks and APIs

Custom websites can post form data straight to a webhook in Zapier, Make, n8n or the CRM itself. This is the most flexible option, and the most likely to break quietly if nobody is watching.

The failure points I fix most often

  • Required fields: the CRM rejects the lead because the form doesn't send a field it requires.
  • Expired connections: a password change disconnects the integration.
  • Silent webhook errors: the website "submits" but nothing arrives.
  • Duplicate contacts from re-submissions.

For one US agency, two client websites were losing form leads before they reached each client's CRM. I rebuilt one flow into JobTread and the other into Zoho, worked around Zoho's required fields and tested end to end with the owner (read the case study).

Always add a safety net

Send an alert when a step fails, and run a weekly check that compares form submissions with new CRM contacts. More in why automations fail quietly.
