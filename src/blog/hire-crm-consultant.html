A CRM consultant can save you months of trial and error, or cost you months if you hire the wrong one. Here's what a good one does, what affects the cost and the questions to ask before you sign.

What a CRM consultant should do

  • Understand how you sell before touching the software.
  • Design pipelines, tags, routing and automations around that process.
  • Move and clean your data safely.
  • Connect your lead sources and tools.
  • Test everything, train your team and document it.

What drives the cost

  1. Data quality: messy data takes longer to migrate and clean.
  2. Integrations: each connected tool adds work.
  3. Number of workflows and how complex they are.
  4. AI components like voice agents or chatbots.
  5. Training and support after launch.

10 questions to ask

  1. Have you worked in my CRM before? Can I see examples?
  2. How will you learn our sales process first?
  3. Is the price fixed, and what exactly is in scope?
  4. How do you prevent duplicates when importing data?
  5. How do you test before going live?
  6. What happens if something breaks after handover?
  7. Will everything be built in my accounts?
  8. Will you use your own user login, not mine?
  9. What documentation and training do we get?
  10. Can I speak to, or read reviews from, past clients?

Red flags

  • No questions about your process, straight to building.
  • Vague scope and hourly billing with no estimate.
  • Building in their own accounts and "renting" the system to you.
  • No testing plan and no documentation.
