A good pipeline answers one question at a glance: where is every deal, and what needs to happen next? Most pipelines I audit fail that test. They have too many stages, vague names and deals that haven't moved in months.

Rule 1: fewer stages than you think

Five to eight stages covers almost every business. Each stage should represent a real change in the buyer's commitment, not an internal admin step.

Rule 2: every stage has an exit rule

Write down what must be true for a deal to move forward. If two people would put the same deal in different stages, the definitions are too loose.

StageExit rule
New leadFirst contact attempted
ContactedTwo-way conversation happened
QualifiedNeed, budget and timeline confirmed
Appointment bookedCall or meeting on the calendar
Proposal sentWritten proposal delivered
Won / LostDecision made, reason recorded

Rule 3: stages for where, tags for what

Stages show progress. Tags and custom fields hold detail: source, service interest, location, price range. Mixing them is how pipelines end up with stages like "Facebook lead – hot – buyer".

Rule 4: design for the real journey

A clinic doesn't sell like an agency. For one clinic I replaced a generic sales pipeline with a patient journey: first appointment, meeting, billing, registration and follow-up visits (read the Zoho case study). Real estate needs nurture and past-client stages because deals can take a year.

Rule 5: automate the moves

  • Form submitted: create the deal in New lead.
  • Appointment booked: move to Appointment booked.
  • Contract signed or payment received: move to Won.
  • No activity for X days: flag or move to nurture.
