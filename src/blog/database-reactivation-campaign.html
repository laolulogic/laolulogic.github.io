Most businesses have hundreds or thousands of leads who asked a question once and then went quiet. They're not dead. They just weren't ready, or nobody followed up. A reactivation campaign starts those conversations again, without spending another dollar on ads.

Here's the process I used to re-engage a 3,287-lead real estate database with 100% delivery and a 42.86% first-touch open rate.

Step 1: audit and clean the list

  • Remove unsubscribed and Do Not Contact records. Non-negotiable.
  • Filter out invalid emails and disconnected numbers. We removed 145 unreachable profiles before sending.
  • Merge duplicates so nobody gets the same message twice.

Step 2: segment

Different leads need different messages. Segment by stage (old lead vs past client), source, timeline and what they originally asked about. Even three segments outperform one generic blast.

Step 3: write messages that start conversations

The goal of the first message is a reply, not a sale. Short and curious works best:

Hi Dan, are you still thinking about selling the place on Oak Avenue this year, or have plans changed?

Follow with three to five touches spaced three to five days apart, then tag non-responders and move them to long-term nurture. My guide to nurture sequences that sound human has more examples.

Step 4: send in stages

Sending thousands of emails at once from a cold list is how domains get flagged as spam. Start small (we started with 151 leads), watch bounces and complaints, then scale up in batches.

Step 5: route hand-raisers instantly

A reply is a hot lead. Route it immediately: financing questions to the lender, ready buyers or sellers to the agent, everyone else back into nurture. Alert the person by text so they call within minutes.

Step 6: add AI calls where it makes sense

For larger databases, an AI agent can call old leads, ask one or two qualifying questions and book appointments for anyone interested, with a clear opt-out. See AI lead qualification.
