Key takeaways

  • A booking agent checks live availability, books, reschedules and cancels.
  • Put it where customers already message you: website chat, WhatsApp or SMS.
  • Give it a small, approved knowledge base and clear limits.
  • Log every booking and conversation in your CRM.

A front desk spends hours a day on the same conversations: "Do you have anything Thursday?", "Can I move my appointment?", "How much is a consultation?". An AI booking agent handles those instantly, on the channels customers already use.

What a booking agent does

  • Answers common questions from approved information.
  • Checks real availability in your calendar.
  • Books, reschedules and cancels appointments.
  • Sends confirmations and reminders.
  • Hands off to a person when needed.

Setup checklist

  1. Knowledge: services, durations, policies, locations. Keep it short and approved.
  2. Calendar rules: availability, buffers and minimum notice.
  3. Guardrails: no invented prices or promises; clear handoff triggers.
  4. Memory: the bot should remember the conversation so customers don't repeat themselves.
  5. CRM logging: contact, booking and conversation summary saved every time.
  6. Testing: realistic conversations, including awkward ones, before launch.

Measure the result

Track bookings made by the bot, handoff rate and time saved. Review conversations weekly at first and tune the instructions. Want one built? It's part of the AI Front Desk package.

Frequently asked questions

Can an AI chatbot really book appointments?

Yes. It connects to your calendar, offers open times, books the slot and sends a confirmation. It can also reschedule and cancel.

Which channels can it work on?

Website chat, WhatsApp, SMS, Facebook and Instagram messages, depending on the platform you use.

What happens if the bot cannot answer a question?

It should say so politely and hand the conversation to a person, with the context attached so the customer does not repeat themselves.
